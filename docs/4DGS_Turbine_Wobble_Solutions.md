# Reconstructing Subtle Motion in 4D Gaussian Splatting: A Solution Architecture for the Turbine-Wobble Collapse

## Executive Summary

The LEGO turbine wobble disappears from the 4DGS reconstruction because three mutually reinforcing mechanisms push the deformation field to the temporal mean: **(1)** all ten cameras share one timestamp per frame index while their true exposure instants differ by 15–25 ms — at the measured 7.11 Hz wobble frequency that is **38–64° of phase disagreement** between views the model believes are simultaneous; **(2)** the 3 000-iteration static coarse stage bakes the time-averaged pose into canonical space, including the densified Gaussian population; **(3)** temporal regularizers and a full-image loss dominated by static background leave a ≤ 3-pixel motion signal with almost no gradient budget. The measured outcome — learned deformation ≈ 0 and motion magnitude that does not even track physical imbalance — is exactly what this combination predicts.

The good news is that every one of these causes lives inside the data pipeline or the model, and the constraint "nothing outside the cameras" eliminates only the least important remedies. The proposed architecture has five layers. **L1, timing recovery:** extract per-camera exposure offsets from the already-recorded RTSP/NTP sidecars (pure arithmetic, zero new code), or — if the sidecars are lost — fit the 7.11 Hz sinusoid independently in each camera's frames and convert relative phase to offset; a single flash of the cameras' built-in LED illuminators resolves the sign/period ambiguity that any purely sinusoidal fit leaves open. **L2, loader:** give each camera its own continuous timestamp tₖ = t + δₖ at `multipleview_dataset.py:54`, following the Sync-NeRF pattern that is now proven on HexPlane-family backbones and on 4DGS itself. **L3, deformation model:** add a Fourier-rigid motion head for the turbine region of interest (a shared 6-DoF body motion parameterized by sinusoids at the known 7.11 Hz and its harmonics), keep the HexPlane for everything else, and stop the coarse stage from densifying on the blurred average. **L4, supervision:** weight the loss toward the moving region (DyNeRF-style temporal-variance weighting, ×20–50), add a trajectory-reprojection loss against the already-measured 2D wobble tracks, and anneal the time-plane regularizers. **L5, validation:** success is defined in the frequency domain — learned trajectories must peak at 7.11 Hz with amplitudes matching the optical tracks (±2.5–3 px at f = 1846 px) and consistent phase across views.

Quantitatively, the signal is comfortably recoverable: at 25 fps the 7.11 Hz wobble is sampled at 3.52 frames per period (0.57 of Nyquist), and even a pessimistic ±25 ms uniform offset spread across ten cameras preserves **80%** of the coherent wobble amplitude — meaning the observed *total* collapse is not an information-theoretic fate but an optimization failure that the five-layer stack directly targets. The single highest-leverage change is **L2 (per-camera timestamps)**; the single cheapest is **L1-sidecar recovery**, which may be one afternoon of file archaeology.

## 1. Problem Synthesis and Design Constraints

The evidence pack diagnoses a rigid, linear, whole-assembly oscillation at 7.11 Hz (the 427 RPM shaft speed) with ±2.5–3 px image amplitude at f = 1846 px, coherent across 55 tracked points to within 0.57 px — and a family of trained 4DGS models whose per-Gaussian trajectory excursions are at the numerical floor (mean max-displacement 0.004–0.01 scene units), with the paradoxical property that the most imbalanced rotor (2 blades missing) learned the *least* motion. That inversion is the signature of the "averaging basin": when views disagree about where the turbine was at the (shared) nominal timestamp, the photometric minimum over the full training set is the temporal mean pose, and the deformation field that encodes "nothing moves" is the only globally consistent hypothesis. This report takes the diagnosis as given and addresses the remedial design space.

Two constraints shape everything that follows. First, **no additional equipment beyond the cameras themselves**: this rules out external sync displays, strobe hardware, motion-capture references, and new sensors, but it does *not* rule out the cameras' own capabilities — the Dahua units in the rig have built-in white-light illuminators triggerable over the existing `rtsp-capture flash` command, remotely lockable shutter/exposure, configurable frame rate, and their RTCP/NTP timestamping path. A flash fired by the camera's own illuminator is, for the purposes of this constraint, a camera feature, not added equipment. Second, **the 4DGS implementation itself is fair game**: loader, deformation model, losses, regularizers, training schedule, and the orchestrator presets may all change. These two constraints are fortunate, because the strongest remedies in the recent literature are precisely in-model changes.

One scope assumption is stated explicitly: the report covers both **recovery from the existing captures** (the three turbine scenes plus `turbine_r1_missing_6`) and a **next-capture protocol** that stays strictly within camera configuration (shutter lock, fps/resolution trade-offs, NTP discipline, flash calibration). The front rotor is treated as a separate sub-problem: it is unrecoverable in the current data because long exposure at 25 fps smears it into a static blur disc, and the honest options are (a) exclude it from motion evaluation, (b) model the blur explicitly in-model, or (c) freeze it next time with a locked short shutter.

## 2. Why the Signal Is Recoverable in Principle

Before prescribing machinery, it is worth establishing that the wobble is not below the information floor of the recordings. The sampling arithmetic is favorable. At 25 fps the frame interval is 40 ms against a 140.6 ms wobble period — **3.52 samples per period**, comfortably below the 12.5 Hz Nyquist limit, so the sinusoid is well-resolved in every camera individually. The damaging quantity is not temporal resolution but *inter-camera* phase disagreement: a per-camera offset Δt translates to a phase error of 360°·f·Δt, i.e. **2.56°/ms at 7.11 Hz**. The measured 15–25 ms residuals therefore scatter the ten views across 38–64° of wobble phase; each view asserts a turbine position up to ~±2.5 px away from what another view asserts at the same nominal time. Averaging ten sinusoids whose phases are uniform in ±δ attenuates the coherent component by sinc(2πfδ): **93% survives at ±15 ms and 80% at ±25 ms**. The collapse to ~0% observed in the trained models is therefore far worse than the information-theoretic bound — the deficit is created by the optimization setup (shared timestamp, static coarse bake, regularizer pressure, background-dominated loss), not by the data.

![Sync offset versus wobble-phase disagreement, and coherent-amplitude attenuation under multi-view averaging](fig_sync_phase_attenuation.png)

Two independent literatures corroborate that motion of this scale is extractable. Vision-based vibration measurement routinely resolves **sub-pixel** periodic displacement from ordinary video: phase-based motion processing represents local motion as phase shifts of complex steerable-pyramid coefficients and amplifies or measures motions far below one pixel ([MIT CSAIL](https://people.csail.mit.edu/nwadhwa/phase-video/)), the original Eulerian approach demonstrated that sub-pixel motion is linearly encoded in per-pixel intensity time series ([ACM](https://cacm.acm.org/research/eulerian-video-magnification-and-analysis/)), and learning-based variants report controlled sub-pixel tests with input motions of **0.01–1 px** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.02684)). Target-free structural displacement methods report per-frame errors around 0.1–0.3 px under low-contrast conditions ([Inria HAL](https://inria.hal.science/hal-04894400/file/Motion_estimation_final.pdf)). The turbine's ±2.5–3 px at training resolution (3200×1800, no downsampling on this path) is an order of magnitude above what these techniques treat as routine — the brief's own §4.1 correctly warns against citing a sub-pixel argument for this dataset.

The second corroboration is the radiance-field literature on **unsynchronized multi-view video**, which has converged on the exact remedy this problem needs. Sync-NeRF introduced continuous per-camera time offsets δₖ optimized jointly with the field, requires no extra loss terms, and recovers ground-truth offsets on synthetic benchmarks to a mean absolute error of roughly **0.01–0.02 s** — and it was demonstrated on K-Planes/HexPlane-style factorized backbones of the same family this repository uses ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.13356v4), [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/28057)). The idea has been carried to 3D Gaussian Splatting explicitly ([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=RQutkn4V9I)), and the 2026 ASTRA framework shows the same joint optimization on **4DGS and EDGS backbones** remaining stable under offsets as severe as 25 frames when photometric alignment is assisted by 2D trajectory supervision, reducing temporal-offset MAE by 54% and nearly quadrupling sync success rate relative to color-only optimization ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02006v3)). The present problem — sub-frame offsets (≤25 ms vs. a 40 ms frame period) with a clean, rigid, periodic signal — is a *mild* instance of the problem these methods solve.

## 3. Layer 1 — Timing Recovery on Existing Data (Camera-Only)

### 3.1 Recover the offsets from the capture sidecars

The fastest path is file archaeology, not algorithm design. The RTSP capture chain timestamps every frame against NTP wall-clock via piecewise-linear interpolation between RTCP Sender Reports, and the misalignment enters only later, at export, where `sync_extract.py` snaps each camera onto a reference timeline by nearest-frame selection with up to ±½ frame (±20 ms) residual. The per-frame NTP tables in the original `recordings/run_*` directories therefore contain, effectively, the ground-truth exposure instants from which all pairwise camera offsets follow by subtraction — no optics, no fitting, no new code. The brief's §4.3 already flags locating these directories as the zero-cost first move; this report upgrades that from a discrepancy note to the **primary recovery path**, with two implementation cautions: verify that the sidecar timeline refers to *sensor frame-start* (not packet-arrival) timestamps, and record exposure duration alongside, because the effective observation instant is mid-exposure, which shifts all cameras by half the (possibly different) exposure times.

If the sidecars cannot be located, the fallback is optical: the wobble itself is a broadcast clock. Every camera observes the same rigid oscillation, so a per-camera 1-D projection of the turbine's motion (centroid of a tracked patch set, or phase of a dominant spatial-frequency band in an ROI) yields ten sinusoids of known common frequency; fitting A·sin(2π·7.11·t + φₖ) per camera and differencing the φₖ recovers relative offsets to a fraction of a millisecond given ~164 frames at 3.52 samples per period, because the phase estimator's variance falls with sample count and the SNR here (3 px signal, sub-pixel noise) is comfortable. This is the same measurement already performed for diagnosis (55 coherent tracked points); wrapping it per camera is a small script in the pattern of the existing `sync_analyzer` robust-fit tooling.

### 3.2 The ambiguity that must be broken — and the flash that breaks it

A purely sinusoidal clock has two structural ambiguities. The phase fit determines each δₖ only **modulo the 140.6 ms wobble period** (3.52 frames), and a rigid linear oscillation has an additional **sign/π-phase ambiguity**: a view delayed by half a period is indistinguishable from one measuring the mirrored motion direction. Integer-period errors are harmless for training only if they are consistent — actually they are not: the HexPlane time axis is shared with all other dynamics, so a one-period slip misaligns that camera's supervision with every other temporal phenomenon in the scene. Both ambiguities are resolved by a single unambiguous common event visible in all cameras, and the rig already owns the tool for it: the cameras' hardware white-light illuminators, addressable per camera via `rtsp-capture flash`. A ~3 s flash sequence appended to a capture (or, for the existing datasets, checked for serendipitous common events — auto-exposure corrections and LED flicker transients sometimes suffice) pins every camera's frame index to one physical instant, converting the phase measurements into absolute offsets. This stays inside the "nothing outside the cameras" constraint, since the illuminator is part of the camera assembly.

Two smaller identifiability points matter for the model side later. First, a **global** time shift of all cameras together is unobservable (the whole scene simply relabels its time axis), so one camera must be anchored at δ = 0 and only nine offsets estimated — the same gauge-fixing used by every bundle-adjustment-style joint estimator from BARF onward. Second, clock **drift** over the 6.6 s capture is worth one check rather than an assumption: the QR-path analyzer in the repo already fits offset *and* drift with ms-level RMSE, and if the sidecars show per-camera rate discrepancies above ~0.1 ms/s, the loader should accept a per-camera affine model tₖ = aₖ·t + δₖ rather than a pure offset. Nothing in the evidence suggests drift matters at this capture length, but the check is free once the phase-fit tooling exists.

## 4. Layer 2 — Per-Camera Timestamps in the Loader

### 4.1 The primary code change

The root defect is a single line: `image_times.append(float(i/image_length))` at `core/scene/multipleview_dataset.py:54`, which asserts that frame *i* of every camera happened at the same instant. The fix is to load a per-camera offset table (from Layer 1) into a small sidecar JSON next to each scene — `{cam01: 0.0, cam02: 0.0073, ...}` in seconds — and evaluate `image_times[k] = (i + δₖ·fps/n_frames_norm)/image_length` in normalized units, or better, to **store fps and physical seconds in the dataset** and normalize once, centrally. The latter simultaneously fixes the §3.6/§4.2–4.3 caveats of the brief: the FFT machinery in `render_amp.py` works in normalized frequency because the dataset discards fps, so every Hz-denominated diagnostic downstream (including the "0.10 Hz" reading nobody could source) becomes well-defined the moment physical time enters the loader. This is a small, reviewable diff: the `Camera` object gains nothing (time stays a per-image scalar), and the HexPlane deformation field needs no change at all — it is already a continuous function of t, which is exactly why Sync-NeRF-style offsets graft onto this backbone without architectural surgery ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.13356v4)).

The expected effect is direct: with corrected timestamps, the ten views stop contradicting each other, the "averaging basin" disappears as a loss minimum, and the 80–93% coherent-amplitude budget of §2 becomes available to the deformation field. It is worth emphasizing that this is the one change that is **not optional**: every other layer in this report (motion-weighted losses, Fourier head, regularizer anneal) operates on a signal that the shared-timestamp bug actively destroys, which is why the brief's measured outcome — motion magnitude anti-correlated with physical imbalance — is the canonical symptom.

### 4.2 Fixed offsets versus learnable refinement

Two operating modes are worth implementing behind the same loader change. In **fixed mode**, the Layer-1 offsets are baked into `image_times` and training is otherwise stock; this is the safest variant and the right first experiment, because it isolates the timing effect with no new optimization dynamics. In **learnable mode**, each δₖ becomes an optimizable parameter (initialized from the Layer-1 estimate, or zero if unavailable), registered in a small dedicated optimizer group with a learning rate well below the deformation field's — Sync-NeRF used 0.1–0.5× the backbone rate and started offset training only after an initial stabilization period of 10k iterations for exactly this reason ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.13356v4)). Learnable refinement matters here for a specific reason: Layer-1 optical estimates are themselves uncertain at the sub-millisecond level, and the training loss is the ultimate arbiter of which offset actually aligns the views.

Learnable offsets carry one real hazard in this scene that the literature warnings map onto cleanly: photometric-only offset optimization can be ambiguous when the dynamic signal is weak, and ASTRA's central finding is that color residuals alone fail to disambiguate synchronization exactly in hard cases — its fix, aligning projected 3D trajectories with observed 2D tracks, is available here in an unusually strong form because the wobble tracks have *already been measured* ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02006v3)). Practically: clamp δₖ to a plausible band (±40 ms), anchor cam01 at zero, and — if the offsets are learnable — add the trajectory-reprojection loss of §6.2 as the disambiguating term. With those three guardrails, the joint estimation is overdetermined and well-behaved; without them, a weak-signal scene invites the offsets to chase noise.

## 5. Layer 3 — Deformation Model: Give the Wobble Somewhere to Live

### 5.1 A Fourier-rigid head for the turbine ROI

The stock decoder asks a width-128, depth-0 MLP over a 64³×200 HexPlane to discover, per Gaussian and from scratch, that ~300 000 points on one rigid assembly all execute the same 7.11 Hz sinusoid ([4D-GS, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08528)). Under conflicting multi-view supervision that discovery never happens; but even *with* fixed timestamps, the parameterization fights the physics — the signal is one rigid body with a handful of numbers (axis, pivot, amplitude, phase, maybe 2–3 harmonics), encoded across hundreds of thousands of independent decoder queries. The literature offers two proven compressions. SC-GS replaces per-Gaussian motion with **sparse control points carrying 6-DoF transformations**, interpolated to Gaussians by learned RBF weights with an as-rigid-as-possible loss; it reports markedly cleaner trajectories than direct per-Gaussian deformation, specifically suppressing the "high-frequency noisy shaking" of the baseline ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.14937), [GitHub](https://github.com/CVMI-Lab/SC-GS)). PVG goes further and makes **periodicity the primitive**: each Gaussian gets a velocity, period, phase, and lifespan, representing all scene dynamics as superposed vibrations ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18561)). A 2026 rigid-body-constrained 4DGS variant demonstrates the extreme end of the same idea — one anchor Gaussian per semantic object, all others updated by relative transformations — with large speed and extrapolation gains ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.25909v2)).

For this scene the recommended form is deliberately the smallest member of that family: a **Fourier-rigid head**, gated to the turbine ROI by a mask (from the existing `motion-seg` clusters or a manual box). All ROI Gaussians share one rigid transform (R(t), T(t)); T(t) is parameterized as Σₙ aₙ·sin(2πn·7.11·t + φₙ) for n = 1..3 along a learnable dominant axis, and R(t) as a small-angle rotation about a learnable pivot — initialized from the measured linear direction. The frequency can be fixed at 7.11 Hz (it is measured to three significant figures) or left learnable with a strong prior. This reduces the wobble hypothesis space from ~10⁶ free parameters to ~20, which both resists the conflicting-gradient regime and makes the motion *auditable*: amplitude, phase, and axis are readable parameters, directly comparable against the optical tracks. Everything outside the ROI — the blur-disc rotor, incidental shake — keeps the stock HexPlane path, so the change is strictly additive.

### 5.2 Coarse-stage de-biasing

The 3 000-iteration static coarse stage is the second structural problem, and it is worse than a wasted warm-up: densification runs during it, so the Gaussian *population* — count, placement, scales — is optimized for the motion-blurred time average, and the fine stage inherits that biased geometry. Three remedies of increasing invasiveness are on the table. The minimal one is to **shorten or skip** the coarse stage (`coarse_iterations` 3 000 → 0–500): with per-camera timestamps fixed, there is no averaging artifact to warm-start away from, and 4DGS-class models routinely train single-stage. The middle option keeps a short coarse stage for the static background but **gates the ROI out of it** — ROI Gaussians query the deformation field from iteration 0, so the canonical turbine pose is anchored at a reference instant rather than the temporal mean; this is a localized change at `core/gaussian_renderer/__init__.py:80-81` and the stage switch in `core/train.py:505-507`. The most principled option initializes canonical space from **one time-consistent multi-view instant** (the frame index whose corrected timestamps cluster tightest across cameras), which gives COLMAP-style geometric consistency to the canonical model.

Whichever variant is chosen, one interaction deserves explicit handling: if ROI densification is deferred to the fine stage, the early fine-stage gradient landscape on the turbine is that of an under-populated Gaussian set, and the Fourier head should be frozen for the first few hundred fine iterations to avoid the motion parameters absorbing geometry errors. This staged unfreezing mirrors the control-point pre-training schedule of SC-GS, which pre-trains motion structure before joint optimization precisely because joint-from-scratch is unstable ([SC-GS supp.](https://yihua7.github.io/SC-GS-web/materials/SC_GS_Supp.pdf)).

## 6. Layer 4 — Supervision: Making 3 Pixels Matter

### 6.1 Motion-mask loss weighting

The turbine occupies a small fraction of 3200×1800 px, so the L1+DSSIM gradient budget at `core/train.py:272-288` is overwhelmingly spent re-rendering static background that is already correct. The canonical remedy predates Gaussian splatting: DyNeRF showed that weighting supervision by **temporal variance** (per-ray residual against the temporal median image, passed through a robust Geman-McClure transfer function, or simple consecutive-frame differences) is necessary to reach acceptable quality on the dynamic regions at all — without it, their model "cannot reach an acceptable visual quality within extended training time" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.02597v2)). In this codebase the change is cheap because the plumbing half-exists: the generic dataloader has a `mask` field (`core/scene/dataset.py:28,38-40`) that is unused on the multipleview path. A temporal-variance weight map per camera (computable offline in one pass over the frames, dilated and clamped to [1, 50]) multiplied into the L1 term implements the brief's "×20–50" proposal with a published recipe behind it.

Two refinements tailor the generic recipe to this scene. Because the motion is periodic and small, the weight map should be computed from **band-passed** frame differences around 6–9 Hz rather than raw differences, which rejects slow auto-exposure drift and lighting flicker that a naive variance map would amplify. And because the background is static *and* textured, a hard ROI rectangle around the turbine with a soft falloff is likely as effective as a learned weight map while being trivially debuggable — the fancy version only earns its complexity if scene two (`turbine_r1_missing_6`) or future captures have motion outside the turbine housing.

### 6.2 Trajectory reprojection supervision

The strongest scene-specific supervision available is the one already in hand: the diagnostic pipeline tracked 55 points on the turbine at sub-pixel coherence. ASTRA demonstrates the general pattern — extract 2D trajectories per view, then penalize the discrepancy between the projected motion of reconstructed 3D points and those tracks, jointly with the temporal offsets; on 4DGS backbones this is what rescues synchronization precisely when photometric cues are too weak ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02006v3)). Here the implementation is simpler than ASTRA's general case because the Fourier-rigid head provides an analytic 3D trajectory for every ROI Gaussian: project the deformed positions into each camera at its corrected timestamp, and L2-align the projected 2D displacement against the measured tracks. The loss simultaneously supervises amplitude, phase, axis, *and* the per-camera offsets, and it does so with a signal that is immune to the appearance-level ambiguities ASTRA criticizes in color-only alignment.

A design warning from the flow-supervision literature applies: supervising *rendered Gaussian flow* against optical-flow fields has a known domain gap — the composed Gaussian motion at a pixel is not a correspondence field — and recent work shows naive flow losses on deformable GS yield surprisingly little motion improvement and can even decouple motion quality from rendering metrics ([DispFlow-GS, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.36940v1); [MotionGS](https://ruijiezhu94.github.io/MotionGS_page/)). The track-reprojection loss recommended here sidesteps the gap because it supervises **displacement of identified points**, not pixel-space flow; if a pixel-level variant is ever added, supervise displacement magnitude maps rather than dense flow correspondence. MD-Splatting's physics-inspired regularizers (local rigidity, momentum conservation, isometry) are the adjacent option if non-rigid rotor motion ever becomes the target — it reports median 3D tracking errors of 3.39 mm on metric-scale cloth under occlusion ([project page](https://md-splatting.github.io/)).

### 6.3 Regularizer schedule and blur handling

The temporal regularizers (`time_smoothness_weight = 1e-3`, `l1_time_planes = 1e-4`, `plane_tv_weight = 2e-4`, applied every fine iteration) are tuned for large, smooth, generic motion and actively suppress a small coherent periodic signal — the brief's recommended values (1e-4 and 0 respectively) are the right direction and are **config-only** via the preset YAMLs. Two refinements: apply the anneal as a *schedule* (start at stock values while geometry stabilizes, decay after iteration ~3 000 of the fine stage) rather than a constant, since early training does benefit from temporal smoothing; and exempt the ROI from `l1_time_planes` entirely once the Fourier head exists, because the head's motion does not live in the time planes at all — penalizing them only distorts the HexPlane's residual degrees of freedom.

The front rotor is the remaining supervised-but-unlearnable element. Two honest options: **mask it out** of both loss and evaluation (declare it unrecoverable at 25 fps with long exposure — at 427 RPM the blade tips move tens of pixels per 40 ms exposure), or adopt the BAD-Gaussians image-formation model, which recovers sharp geometry from motion-blurred inputs by learning a camera/scene motion trajectory *within* the exposure interval and integrating the render over it ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.11831v2)); the same physical-formation modeling has been shown to handle blur and rolling-shutter jointly in the GS framework ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.13327v3)) with NeRF-side lineage in USB-NeRF's spline-based exposure modeling ([ICLR](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/f606d45ae7b991988b6eea2af38b7057-Abstract-Conference.html)). For the rotor, exposure-integration is the *correct* model but a poor investment: it buys a deblurred disc whose motion is still not identifiable at this blur level. Mask-and-exclude is the recommendation; the real rotor fix is the shutter lock in §10.

## 8. Recommended Implementation Sequence

The program falls naturally into four phases, ordered so that the cheapest high-information steps come first and every phase ends with a retrain whose result is interpretable. The prioritization below is judgement-based (no benchmark exists for this exact configuration), but the ordering logic — recover timing information before spending model capacity, add capacity only where structure is known — follows directly from the failure analysis.

![Effort–impact prioritization of the proposed fixes](fig_priority_matrix.png)

**Phase 0 — Timing recovery (days, no GPU).** Locate the `recordings/run_*` sidecars and compute the offset table (R3). In parallel, implement `track_wobble_phase.py` (R4) as the fallback and cross-check: the two estimates agreeing to within ~2 ms is itself a strong validation of both. If neither is possible on existing data, schedule the 3-second flash capture (R5) with the current rig configuration. Deliverable: `offsets.json` per scene, stored in `data/multipleview/<scene>/`, containing per-camera offset, exposure, and fps — which also permanently fixes the normalized-frequency FFT caveat.

**Phase 1 — Config and loss quick wins (one retrain).** R1 + R2 + R10 together, still on shared timestamps. Expected outcome: partial recovery at best (the sync conflict is untouched), but the run establishes the new baselines and exercises the mask plumbing. If even this run shows nonzero structured deformation at 7.11 Hz, the coherent-amplitude budget (§2) is confirmed empirically.

**Phase 2 — The timestamp fix (the pivotal retrain).** R6 with fixed offsets from Phase 0. This is the experiment the whole program hinges on; run it on `lego_turbine_2_missing` first (the imbalance case with the most collapsed baseline). Success criterion: learned trajectory FFT peaks at 7.11 Hz. Then add R7 (learnable refinement, anchored, clamped) and R8 (trajectory loss) as a second run. Expected outcome per the literature: offsets refined to ~0.01 s accuracy or better are routine for this pattern ([Sync-NeRF](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.13356v4)), and with only ±25 ms of true offset the problem is far milder than the published benchmarks.

**Phase 3 — Structured motion (the fidelity retrain).** R9 (Fourier-rigid head) on top of Phase 2, initialized from the Phase-2 deformation. This phase converts a recovered-but-noisy deformation field into a clean, auditable, physically parameterized motion — the form in which "capture even subtle movements" becomes a *measurement* capability (amplitude/phase/axis per scene) rather than a rendering quality metric. Compare base vs. 2-missing vs. 5-missing wobble amplitude as the first physics sanity check: amplitude should now *increase* with imbalance, inverting the pathological ordering of the current models.

### 8.1 Ablation discipline

With 11 candidate changes, combinatorics is the enemy; the ablation ladder should be run as a strict sequence where each run toggles exactly one factor against the best-known configuration, in the order R6 → R2 → R1 → R10 → R7 → R8 → R9. Every run logs, beyond PSNR/SSIM: the learned per-camera offsets (when applicable), the ROI-averaged trajectory amplitude and its FFT peak, and the cross-view phase coherence metric of §9. Two anti-patterns are worth naming explicitly because they are the ones this codebase tempts: do not change regularizer weights and loss masking in the same run (R1 and R2 interact — a stronger motion signal makes the regularizers bite harder); and do not judge motion recovery by rendered-image metrics alone — the DispFlow-GS authors document that motion fidelity and image metrics can decouple, so image PSNR is a guardrail here, not the objective ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.36940v1)).

A second discipline concerns bookkeeping rather than run design: every configuration change in the ladder should land as a named orchestrator preset (`orchestrator/pipeline/config/presets/lego_turbine_*.yaml`), so the ladder is reproducible from version control rather than from shell history, and each retrain should register its `manifest.json` with the offset table hash it consumed. This matters because the offset table is itself an evolving artifact through Phase 0–2 — a run trained against `offsets.json@rev3` is not comparable to one against `@rev1` unless the manifests say so. Given that the four turbine scenes share rig geometry, one final leverage point: offsets fitted on one scene are a prior for the others (same cameras, same network), so the ladder can propagate timing knowledge across scenes and reserve per-scene fitting as a refinement, roughly tripling the effective data behind each offset estimate.

## 9. Validation Protocol: Defining "Captured" in the Frequency Domain

The current evaluation (`runs/lego_turbine_evaluation_summary.csv`) measures per-Gaussian trajectory excursion — the right family of metric, but blind to *structured* motion: a field of incoherent jitter and a true 7.11 Hz oscillation can produce similar excursion distributions. The validation set for this program should therefore be frequency- and phase-aware. Concretely, five metrics, all computable from the existing `trajectories.npz` artifacts: **(1)** spectral peak test — the ROI-averaged displacement trajectory must show a dominant peak at 7.11 Hz (now expressible in physical Hz, since Phase 0 stores fps); **(2)** amplitude fidelity — spectral amplitude at 7.11 Hz, projected through the camera intrinsics, must match the optically measured ±2.5–3 px within a tolerance band (suggest ±30%); **(3)** cross-view phase coherence — the wobble phase implied by the model at each camera's corrected timestamps must be consistent across all ten views (this directly re-tests the §3.1 defect); **(4)** offset accuracy — where ground truth exists (flash event or sidecar arithmetic), |δ̂ₖ − δₖ| per camera, with ≤2 ms (≈5°) as the pass bar; **(5)** rendering guardrail — held-out-view PSNR/SSIM must not regress versus the static-collapse baseline, protecting against the known failure mode where motion losses buy trajectory quality at the expense of appearance.

A synthetic pre-flight test is strongly recommended before burning real retrains: render (or hand-construct) a toy multi-view sequence with a known 7.11 Hz rigid oscillation, inject the measured 15–25 ms offsets, and verify that the modified pipeline recovers the injected motion and offsets end-to-end. This costs one synthetic scene and converts every later ambiguous result into a diagnostic — if the synthetic case fails, the bug is in the implementation; if it passes and the real case fails, the gap is in the data assumptions (exposure, drift, non-rigidity). The brief's own `pump01` synthetic pipeline and `docs/capture_limitation_readouts.md` pre-registration habit are the obvious substrate for this.

## 10. Next-Capture Protocol (Camera-Only)

Everything here uses capabilities the Dahua rig already exposes; nothing is added to the scene. **Shutter lock** across all cameras (`rtsp-capture config --all --shutter 1/…`) is the single most valuable change: at 1/100 s (10 ms), the wobble's peak image-plane velocity of ~134 px/s contributes ~1.3 px of intra-exposure blur — comparable to the motion itself and fully compatible with sharp reconstruction — while the front rotor still blurs; at 1/250–1/500 the rotor becomes reconstructable in principle, at the cost of gain/noise that the 6 MP sensors will feel indoors. The frame-rate decision is a real trade, not a free upgrade: 50–60 fps roughly doubles temporal oversampling of the 7.11 Hz signal (7+ samples/period) and halves nearest-frame alignment residuals to ±10 ms, but Dahua cameras typically pay for it in resolution, and the repo's own capture-limitation study concluded that pixel density and texture — not amplitude — are the binding constraints on motion detectability; at 7.11 Hz the current 25 fps is already adequate, so **keep resolution, lock shutter** is the recommended prioritization.

On timing, the capture-side ceiling should be understood honestly: NTP on a disciplined LAN delivers ±2–5 ms typically and ±1–10 ms broadly ([OpenIPC wiki](https://github.com/OpenIPC/wiki/blob/master/en/timestamps-and-time-sync.md), [ntp.org](https://www.ntp.org/reflib/ptp/)) — i.e. 5–26° of wobble phase — so NTP alone will never satisfy the ≤2 ms target that clean multi-view consistency wants; PTP with hardware timestamping reaches sub-microsecond but only if the cameras' PHYs support it, which commodity SoCs generally do not ([OpenIPC wiki](https://github.com/OpenIPC/wiki/blob/master/en/timestamps-and-time-sync.md)). The practical conclusion: **do not chase network timing — measure, don't synchronize**. The flash-calibration step (fire each camera's illuminator briefly at capture start and end; fit offset *and drift*) reduces per-camera timing error to the phase-fit noise floor (well under 1 ms with ~150+ frames), costs nothing, requires no new hardware, and — importantly — produces a *documented, per-capture* timing certificate that travels with the dataset, so no future analysis ever again has to rediscover the sync state of a scene from forensic tracking. A redundant belt-and-braces option is deliberately *staggering* the cameras' phase on the reference timeline rather than snapping them: asynchronous interleaved sampling increases the rig's effective temporal sampling of the wobble tenfold and is free once offsets are measured rather than assumed.

## 11. Risks, Open Questions, and Failure Modes

The dominant technical risk is **sidecar loss**: if `recordings/run_*` is gone and no common event exists in the existing footage, the optical phase fit leaves the modulo-period and sign ambiguities of §3.2 unresolved, and the flash fix requires a re-shoot. Mitigation if re-shooting is impossible: train with all 2ᴷ sign/period hypotheses over a coarse grid (10 cameras → a manageable subset after anchoring and clustering the phase fits) and select by held-out-view loss — inelegant but bounded, and the Fourier head's tiny parameter count makes each hypothesis cheap to test. The second risk is **identifiability under a rigid linear oscillation**: a purely 1-D wobble observed from a ring of cameras has a weakly observed component along the motion axis itself; the trajectory loss (R8) with tracks spread across the visible turbine surface is the specific countermeasure, and a residual degeneracy would show up immediately in validation metric (2) as a systematic amplitude underestimate.

Remaining open questions are narrower. Clock drift within a capture is assumed negligible but unmeasured — answered for free by the offset+drift fit in Phase 0. Whether 7.11 Hz has detectable harmonics (blade-pass frequencies, structural resonances) determines whether the Fourier head needs n > 3 — answered by the existing per-camera FFTs. The 164-vs-160-vs-261 frame-count discrepancy (brief §4.4) must be reconciled before the phase fits are trusted, since a 4-frame drop shifts absolute timing by 160 ms — more than a full wobble period. And the exposure time per camera is currently unrecorded; mid-exposure correction shifts all offsets by half an exposure and matters at the 2 ms accuracy target. None of these block Phase 0; all of them live in its deliverable checklist. The honest bottom line: the path from "static reconstruction" to "7.11 Hz, ±3 px, auditable rigid-body motion in 4DGS" runs through one afternoon of timestamp archaeology, one loader diff, and three disciplined retrains — with every subsequent improvement (learnable offsets, trajectory loss, Fourier head) being incremental insurance rather than a prerequisite.
